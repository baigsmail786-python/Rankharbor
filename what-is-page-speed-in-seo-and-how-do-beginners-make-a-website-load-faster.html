What Page Speed Means in SEO

Page speed measures how fast a page loads and becomes usable, and Google reads it as a ranking signal. A slow page loses visitors before they read a word, and a fast page keeps them long enough to act. Google measures speed with Core Web Vitals, and those scores describe loading, interactivity, and visual stability in plain terms. You fix speed because the visitor feels it first, and the ranking follows the visitor.

How Google Reads Speed as a Ranking Signal

Google treats speed as one ranking signal among many, and it weighs it most when two pages match on quality. A fast page wins the tie, because Google wants the searcher to land on a page that works. Google measures the experience with Core Web Vitals, and it reads the scores from real visitors on real devices. A page that loads fast on a cheap phone beats a page that only loads fast on a fast office connection.

How Slow Pages Lose Visitors

A visitor decides in seconds whether to stay or leave, and a slow page spends that time loading instead of showing the answer. A shopper who waits for a product page often taps the back button and opens the next result. A reader who waits for a blog post does the same, and the visit never turns into a signup. A bakery site that cut its image sizes saw visitors stay longer, because the menu appeared before their patience ran out.

Measure Your Speed Before You Fix Anything

You measure speed before you fix it, because the report tells you which fix matters most. You open Google PageSpeed Insights, you paste your page URL, and you read the score for mobile first. You note the score, the largest contentful paint, and the list of issues the tool flags. You learn how to improve page speed for SEO beginners by fixing the top issue first, and you measure again after each fix.

Run PageSpeed Insights and Read the Report

You open PageSpeed Insights, and you paste the URL of the page you want to test. You read the mobile score first, because most visitors arrive on a phone. You scroll to the list of opportunities, and you read each one in plain words, because the tool names the fix beside the problem. A score in the red tells you the page needs work, and a score in the green tells you to look at the next page instead.

Record the Score So You Can Compare Later

You write the score and the date in a simple sheet, and you add the top three issues the tool flagged. You record the same numbers after each fix, and the sheet shows which fix moved the score. You test the homepage and your most visited page, because those two pages carry the most traffic. A notebook with dates and scores beats memory, because it proves which fix worked and which one changed nothing.

The Fixes That Move the Score Most

A few fixes move the score more than all the rest, and you start with those. You compress every image, you cut the scripts that block the page, and you turn on caching so repeat visits load fast. You remove the plugins and the widgets you never use, because each one adds weight to the page. You learn how to improve page speed for SEO beginners by fixing the biggest drag first, and you watch the score climb after each change.

Compress and Resize Every Image

Images carry most of the weight on a typical page, and a photo straight from a camera can weigh several megabytes. You resize each image to the size it shows on the page, and you compress it before you upload it. You use a modern format where your platform supports it, and you add lazy loading so images below the fold load only when the reader scrolls to them. Read How Do Beginners Optimize Images So a Website Loads Faster for the image routine that cuts page weight fast.

Cut Render Blocking Scripts and Enable Caching

A script that blocks rendering stops the page from showing until the script finishes, and the visitor stares at a blank screen. You defer the scripts that can wait, and you remove the ones no page uses. You turn on browser caching, and the browser stores the files so a repeat visit loads in a fraction of the time. You also cut third party widgets you never use, because each one adds a request the page must wait for.

The Monthly 15 Minute Speed Check

Speed slips back over time, because every new image, plugin, and widget adds weight to the page. You run a short check each month, and you catch the slowdown before it costs you visitors. You test the same pages each time, and you compare the scores with your sheet. You fix the new drag at once, and the routine keeps the site fast all year.

Repeat the Same Check Every Month

You pick one day each month, and you test the homepage and your most visited page in PageSpeed Insights. You write the new scores beside the old ones, and you read the new list of issues. You fix the biggest new drag first, and you leave the small ones for the next round. A monthly check takes fifteen minutes, and it stops a slow creep that a yearly audit would miss.

Catch a Slowdown Before It Costs You Traffic

A slowdown shows first in the score, and it shows later in the traffic, so the score gives you an early warning. You watch for a new plugin, a new image size, or a new script that arrived since the last check. You remove the cause at once, and you test again to confirm the score recovered. What is a good page speed score?
