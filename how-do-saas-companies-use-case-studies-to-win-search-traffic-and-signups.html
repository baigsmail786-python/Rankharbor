You build the page in three parts, and each part answers one question the reader asks. The problem part names the pain the customer felt, and it uses the words the customer used. The solution part shows how the product fixed the pain, and it names the features that did the work. The results part shows what changed, and it uses honest numbers from the customer.

What is a SaaS case study?

A SaaS case study is a page that tells how one customer used a software product to solve a real problem. It shows the problem, the fix, and the result in plain words, and it uses the customer words where it can. You use it to win search traffic and trial signups from buyers who want proof.

What should a SaaS case study page include?

A case study page should include the customer type, the problem, the solution, and the result, and each part needs its own heading. You add a quote from the customer in their own words, and you add a trial button near the result. You link the page to your product pages, and you keep the story honest and short.

A case study should run long enough to tell the full story, and most pages need five to eight short paragraphs to do it well. You keep each part focused on one question, and you cut any line that adds no proof. You let the result set the length, and a small win needs fewer words than a big one.
