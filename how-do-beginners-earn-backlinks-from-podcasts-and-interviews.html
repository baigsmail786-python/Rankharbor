How Do Beginners Earn Backlinks From Podcasts and Interviews?

Learn how beginners earn backlinks from podcasts and interviews. Find shows in your niche, pitch a clear angle, and turn one appearance into a show notes link.

Short answer: You earn a backlink from a podcast when you appear as a guest and the host links to your site in the show notes. You learn how to earn backlinks from podcasts for beginners by finding small shows, pitching one clear angle, and making the link easy to add.

Most beginners pitch big shows with a vague note about themselves and hear nothing back. Hosts delete those pitches fast because they offer no clear topic for listeners. That habit wastes a strong link source.

You finish with a pitch email, a list of shows to contact, and a plan for the link after the show airs.

Learn How a Podcast Link Works

A podcast link lives on the episode page, where the host lists the guest, the topics, and the links mentioned in the show. A listener taps that link and lands on your site, while Google reads the link as a vote from the show page. A small business owner who talks about pricing on a local podcast can earn a link from the episode page. That link helps rankings and sends listeners who already trust the host.

Show Notes Carry the Link You Want

Show notes are the written page for an episode, and most hosts add links to the guest site there. A guest who mentions a free guide during the show gives the host a clear reason to link to it. A guest who only talks about himself gives the host nothing to link to. Mention one useful page by name during the show, and the host can add the link without extra work.

Dofollow and Nofollow Links Both Help a Guest

A show notes link may carry a dofollow tag or a nofollow tag, and the host decides which one it gets. A dofollow link passes ranking trust, while a nofollow link still sends listeners and keeps your link profile natural. A beginner who wins a nofollow link from a real show still gains readers who trust the host. Chase the appearance first and let the tag follow, because the listeners matter as much as the link.

Find Small Podcasts That Welcome New Guests

Small podcasts welcome new guests far more often than big shows, because small hosts need fresh voices each week. A show with a few hundred listeners still gives you a link and an audience that cares about the topic. A plumber who finds three local home shows can pitch all three in one afternoon. Start small, win the first appearance, and let that episode open the door to bigger shows.

Search Google and Apple Podcasts for Your Niche

Type your topic beside the word podcast into Google and Apple Podcasts, and list the shows that match your niche. A search for gardening podcast returns shows that talk to gardeners every week. A search for small business podcast returns shows that welcome owners as guests. Write each show name, the host name, and the show link in a sheet, and work through the list one show at a time.

Check Past Guests and Show Notes Before You Pitch

Open a show page and read the last few episodes to see whether the host links to guests. A show that links to every guest will likely link to you too. A show that never links to guests will waste your pitch, so skip it and move to the next show. Pitch the Host With a Short Email That Works

A good pitch email reads like a note from a listener, not a sales letter from a stranger. Open with the episode you heard, name the angle you can teach, and close with your name and your site link. A host who reads a pitch that names a real episode knows you listened. That personal touch earns more replies than a long list of your wins.

Copy This Pitch Email and Change the Details

A short pitch email follows one shape, and you can copy it for every show you contact. The email opens with the host name and the episode you heard, then it names the one angle you can teach in plain words. A dentist could write that she loved the episode on teeth whitening and can share three mistakes patients make before they book, with a guide on her site that covers each one. The email closes with one line that offers three dates for the recording, and that shape keeps the ask small and clear.

Pitch With No Audience and No Credentials

A beginner with no audience can still win a guest spot, because hosts need useful angles more than famous names. Offer one clear lesson from your own work, such as how you fixed a real problem for a real client. A new plumber who shares three leak fixes from his own jobs teaches more than a famous name with no fresh story. You learn how to earn backlinks from podcasts for beginners when you pitch the lesson, not your name.

Turn One Appearance Into Links That Last

One podcast appearance can earn more than one link when you follow up with care. Thank the host, share the episode with your readers, and ask politely for the link when it goes live. A guest who shares the episode gives the host new listeners, and the host remembers that guest. That goodwill turns one show into repeat invites and more links.

Follow Up Once When the Link Never Appears

Some hosts air the episode yet forget to add the link, so follow up once with a kind note. Wait three days after the episode airs, then send one short email that thanks the host and asks for the link to your guide. A host who missed the link will usually add it after that nudge. Never send a second reminder about the same link, because pressure turns a warm host cold.

Repurpose the Episode Into More Links

One episode can earn links beyond the show notes when you turn it into content of your own. Write a short post that sums up the three lessons you shared, and link to the episode from that post. Other sites that read your post may link to it, and the host may share your post with the show audience. Frequently Asked Questions

Do podcast backlinks help SEO?

Yes, a link from a show notes page passes trust when the show covers your topic. The link also sends listeners who already trust the host. Both the ranking help and the warm traffic make the appearance worth the effort.

How do I find podcasts in my niche?

Search Google and Apple Podcasts for your topic beside the word podcast. List the shows that match your niche, then check their past guests and show notes. Work through the list one show at a time.

Should I ask the host for a dofollow link?

You can ask politely for a link to your guide, yet the host decides the tag. A dofollow link passes more trust, while a nofollow link still sends listeners. Chase the appearance first and let the tag follow.

What if I have no audience or credentials?

Hosts need useful angles more than famous names, so pitch one clear lesson from your own work. A beginner who shares three real fixes from real jobs teaches more than a famous name with no fresh story. Anyone who asks how to earn backlinks from podcasts for beginners should pitch the lesson, not the name.

How long should my pitch email be?

Keep the pitch to three short paragraphs at most, and name one real episode in the first lines. Hosts read short mail and delete long pitches without a second thought. A short note that shows you listened earns more replies than a long list of wins.

What do I do when the host never adds my link?

Wait three days after the episode airs, then send one short email that thanks the host and asks for the link. Most hosts add the link after that one nudge. Never send a second reminder about the same link.

Can I host my own podcast to earn backlinks?

Yes, hosting your own show can earn links when guests share the episode and link to it. The show needs real listeners before guests will share it. Guesting on other shows works faster for a beginner.

How many podcast appearances should a beginner aim for?

Aim for one appearance a month to start, because each show brings a link and a new audience. Three shows in your niche give you three links and three groups of listeners. Anyone who learns how to earn backlinks from podcasts for beginners should grow the list slowly and keep each pitch personal.
