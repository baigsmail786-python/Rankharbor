LSI Means Words That Share a Context

LSI stands for latent semantic indexing, and it is an old method that finds words which appear together because they share a context. A page about apples that uses the words pie, tree, and fruit talks about the fruit, while a page that uses computer, OS, and iPhone talks about the company. You use related words to tell Google which meaning you mean, and the page lands in front of the right readers. You think of LSI keywords as context clues, and you use them to clear up ambiguity.

LSI Keywords Are Not Synonyms

LSI keywords are not synonyms, because a synonym means the same thing and an LSI keyword only shares the context. The words baked apples and red apples are not synonyms, yet they both tell Google that the page is about the fruit. You use related words that a real expert would use, and you never force a synonym into a sentence that does not need it. You write about the topic fully, and the related words appear on their own.

Google Does Not Use LSI Technology

Google has stated that it does not use latent semantic indexing to rank pages, because the method is old and built for small sets of documents. Google uses newer methods such as natural language processing and knowledge graphs to understand a page. You still use related words, because they help Google understand your topic and they help readers trust your page. You call them semantic keywords when you want the accurate name, and the habit stays the same.

Place Related Words Naturally in Your Article

You place related words naturally in your article, because a forced word breaks the flow and tells the reader you wrote for a machine. You use a related word when the sentence needs it, and you leave it out when the sentence stands without it. You place related words in the body of the article, because words in the body carry more weight than words in a header or a footer. A writer in Karachi places her related words this way, and her pages read like a person wrote them.

Cover Subtopics Instead of Repeating Words

You cover the subtopics that a real answer needs, and the related words appear as you explain each one. You write a section on each subtopic, and you let the words that belong there appear in that section. You never list related words in a row, because a list of words is not an answer. You write for the reader first, and the search engine reads the same page you wrote.

Keep Related Words Close to the Main Keyword

You keep related words close to your main keyword in the same sentence or the same paragraph, because closeness shows Google that the words belong together. You write a sentence that uses the main keyword and one related word together, and the connection is clear. You spread the related words through the article, and you never crowd them into one paragraph. You read the page aloud once, and you cut any word that sounds forced.
