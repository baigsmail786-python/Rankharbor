Create a Free Account and Type Your First Seed Word

You create a free AnswerThePublic account and you pick one seed word for the session. A free account gives you a small number of searches each day and that limit keeps a beginner focused. You choose a seed word that names a topic you know well.

You type the seed word into the search box and you set your country and language before you run the search. The tool reads the word and it builds a wheel of questions around it. You read the wheel once and you learn the shape of the questions before you save any of them.

Ayesha runs a small bakery blog in Lahore and she creates a free AnswerThePublic account on a Monday. She types bread baking into the search box and she sets Pakistan as her country. The wheel shows questions about soft naan and sourdough starter and she saves the ones that fit her posts.

Read the Keyword Wheel and Sort the Questions

how to use AnswerThePublic for keyword research means you read the keyword wheel in groups and not as one long list. The wheel groups the questions by words like how and what and why and when. You open one group at a time and you read the questions that sit inside it.

You sort the questions into three piles in a simple sheet and you name the piles write now and write later and skip. A question lands in the write now pile when it matches a post you can write this week. A question lands in the skip pile when it names a topic you know nothing about.

Bilal manages a clothing store site in Karachi and he types winter shawls into the search box. He opens the how group first and he finds a question about how to wash a wool shawl at home. He moves that question to his write now pile and he plans a care guide around it.

Save the Questions That Match Posts You Can Write

You save only the questions that match posts you can write with real skill. A saved question must name a clear need and it must fit a page that your site still lacks. You copy the question into your sheet with the seed word beside it.

You check each saved question against the posts that your site already holds. A question that your site already answers in full goes into the skip pile. You keep the list short so your next writing session starts with a clear choice.

Sana writes travel guides in Faisalabad and she saves three questions about hill stations in winter. She checks the questions against her posts and she drops one that her packing guide already answers. She keeps two questions and she plans one guide for each of them.

Turn One Question Into an Article and Repeat the Routine

You turn one saved question into one article and you answer the question in the first lines. You open with the direct answer and you follow it with the steps that the reader needs. You add a real example so the reader trusts the advice on the page.

You build a weekly routine that keeps your question work short and steady. You run one AnswerThePublic search each week and you pick one seed word per session. You save the best questions in a sheet and you write one article from that sheet each week.
