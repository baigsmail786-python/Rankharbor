We Sent 594 Cold Emails for SEO Clients. Here’s What Actually Happened.

Published 14 November 2026 • Data: 4–13 November 2026 • RankHarbor original research

Between 4 and 13 November 2026, RankHarbor sent 594 cold emails to small businesses in the UK, the US and Canada. Every email offered the same thing: a free SEO audit. No paid ads, no bought lists, no purchased links. This page shares the real numbers — how many replied, how many bounced, what they asked for, and what it cost. If you run a small agency or a small business, these numbers are useful because they come from real sends, not from a screenshot in a sales video.

How we sent them

We sent 50 emails a day from rankharbor67@gmail.com. Every business was verified first: a live website and an email address published on that business’s own site. Each email named the business, offered a free SEO audit, and included a one-line opt-out. We attempted 600 sends in total; 6 timed out and were never confirmed, so they are not counted as sent. The 594 below are confirmed in our Sent folder, one by one.

What we learned

1. Most replies were sellers, not buyers

11 of the 14 replies offered to sell us something — paid guest posts or paid link placements, priced from $25 to $350 per post, with a median price of $95. Only one reply was a business that wanted to buy SEO services. If you do cold outreach, expect most replies to be people selling to you.

2. The one real client asked for proof first

A roofing company in Toronto replied that it was interested only in pay-for-performance, and asked for our price after payment and a sample report. Small businesses do not buy promises; they buy evidence. We sent sample findings and our prices, with no ranking guarantees, and offered the full free audit if they said yes.

3. The wrong audience gives the wrong replies

On 12 November, 16 of our 50 emails went to SEO blogs and SEO tools with a guest-post pitch instead of a services pitch. The replies that came back were about guest posts, not clients. We corrected the pitch the same day: businesses that need SEO only, services pitch only. One wrong audience wastes a whole day’s batch.

4. Bounces are a data-quality tax

9 of 594 emails bounced (1.5%) — dead domains, full mailboxes, misconfigured servers. Every address is now verified before sending, and every bounced address is removed from future lists.

5. Free editorial still exists

One publisher accepted a free guest post with an outline first and no payment, and another site asked us to complete a free collaboration form. Paid is the default ask, but it is not the only option. We never pay for placements.

What this means for you

If you send cold email for your own business, expect about 2 replies in 100 — and expect most of them to try to sell you something. The reply that matters is the one that asks for proof. Send a real audit, a real sample and a real price. Check your list before you send, pitch the right audience, and keep every number honest. That is how a small agency earns its first client.

Limits of this data

This is 10 days of data from one small agency, one offer (a free audit) and one sender address. Reply rates change with the audience, the offer and the season. We will update this page as the numbers grow.
