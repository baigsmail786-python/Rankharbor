How Do Beginners Find Competitor Backlinks They Can Copy?

Find competitor backlinks with free tools only. Build a shortlist of links worth copying, then turn it into an outreach list that wins real links.

Short answer: You find competitor backlinks by entering a rival domain into a backlink tool and reading the list of sites that link to it. Free tools show the list, and you need no paid plan to start. You learn how to find competitor backlinks for beginners by copying the links that fit your site and skipping the rest.

Most beginners copy every link a competitor has, and they chase links that do not fit their topic. They then waste weeks on links that never lift rankings.

You will build a free workflow and shortlist the best links for outreach. You will then win links that fit your site.

Why Competitor Backlinks Make a Good Starting Point

A competitor backlink is a link that points to a rival site, and it shows you a page that already links to sites like yours. You start with these links because the site owner has already said yes to your topic once. A fresh search for link targets takes days, and a competitor list gives you proven targets in minutes. You treat the list as a map, and you walk it one link at a time instead of guessing where to go.

A Competitor Link Shows a Page That Accepts Links

A page that links to your competitor has already linked out to a site in your niche, and that fact makes it a warm target. You read the linking page and see what kind of content earns a link there, and you shape your pitch around that pattern. A directory page links to many sites, and a resource page links only to the best guides. You match your page to the pattern, and your pitch lands on a page that already links out.

Shared Links Reveal the Easiest Wins

A link that points to two or three of your competitors is the easiest win on the list, because several rivals already earned it. You sort the list by the number of competitors each domain links to, and the shared links rise to the top. A small SaaS blog in Lahore found twelve shared links this way, and it won four of them in a month with simple pitches. You start with shared links, and you save the hard targets for later.

Find Competitor Backlinks With Free Tools Only

You learn how to find competitor backlinks for beginners fastest with free tools, because a small site needs no paid plan to read a rival list. You open the free backlink checker from Ahrefs and type in a competitor domain, and the tool shows the top links with no account. You open the free version of another tool when the first one limits the list, and you combine both lists in one sheet. You then pick the strongest competitor in your niche, and you run the same check on two more rivals.

Start With the Free Backlink Checker

You open the free backlink checker and type the domain of your strongest competitor, and the tool returns the pages that link to it. You read the list from the top, and you note the domain, the linking page, and the anchor text for each row. You skip links from pages that look like spam, and you keep links from pages that read like real content. You save the list in a sheet, and you add a column for the outreach status of each link.

List the Domains That Link to Two Competitors

You run the same check on a second competitor, and you compare the two lists side by side in your sheet. You mark every domain that appears on both lists, and those shared domains become your first targets. You add a third competitor when the shared list looks thin, and the overlap grows with each new rival. You stop at three competitors, because more lists add noise faster than they add wins.

Sort the List and Keep Only the Links Worth Chasing

A raw list holds hundreds of links, and most of them are not worth your time. You sort the list by relevance first, because a link from your niche helps more than a link from a random site. You check the linking page for real readers, and you drop pages that exist only to sell links. You then rank the rest by traffic and authority, and you keep the top twenty as your shortlist.

Score Each Link by Relevance First

You score each link by asking whether the linking page covers your topic, and you keep only the pages that pass that test. You read the page title and the first paragraph, and you judge the fit in seconds. A link from a baking blog helps a bakery site, and a link from a car forum does not. You mark the fit in your sheet, and you sort the list so the best fits rise to the top.

Drop Links That Hurt More Than Help

You drop links from link farms, paid networks, and sites that Google has clearly left behind, because they hurt more than they help. You check the site for real posts, real comments, and recent updates, and you remove any site that fails the check. You also drop links from pages that no longer exist, because a dead page sends no value. Read What Is Broken Link Building to learn how dead pages turn into link chances of their own.

Turn the Shortlist Into an Outreach List

A shortlist becomes an outreach list when you add a person and a reason to each row. You visit each linking page and find the name of the editor or the site owner, and you note the email or the contact form. You read one recent article on the site, and you find a real detail to mention in your pitch. You then write a short pitch that offers a better page or a fresh angle, and you send it to one site at a time.

Find the Right Page and the Right Person

You find the right person by reading the about page and the author box, and you note the name and the role of the person who edits the site. You check the contact page for an email, and you use the form only when no email shows. You never pitch a generic inbox with a generic message, because a named person answers far more often. A freelancer in Karachi found the editor name on every site she pitched, and her reply rate doubled in a month.

Write a Pitch That Offers Real Value

You write a pitch that opens with one real detail from the site, and you name the article you read and what you liked in it. You then offer a page of yours that adds to the topic, and you say where the link would help the reader. You keep the pitch under one hundred words, and you close with a simple question that invites a yes or a no. Frequently Asked Questions

What are competitor backlinks?

Competitor backlinks are the links that point to a rival site in your niche, and they show you pages that already link to sites like yours. You find them by entering a rival domain into a backlink tool, and the tool returns the list of linking pages. You copy the links that fit your site, and you skip the ones that do not.

Can I find competitor backlinks for free?

You can find competitor backlinks with free tools, and the free backlink checker from Ahrefs shows the top links for any domain. You run the check on two or three competitors, and you combine the lists in one sheet. You need no paid plan to start, and you add a paid tool only when the free lists run dry.

Which competitor should I check first?

You check the strongest competitor in your niche first, because its links show what Google already rewards. You pick a rival that ranks for the keywords you want, and you read its top pages before you check its links. You then check two more rivals, and you mark the links that appear on more than one list.

How do I know which competitor backlinks are worth copying?

You judge a link by relevance first, and you keep only the pages that cover your topic. You check the linking page for real readers and recent posts, and you drop link farms and paid networks. You then rank the rest by traffic, and you keep the top twenty as your shortlist.

What is a link gap report?

A link gap report shows the domains that link to your competitors but not to you, and it finds the shared links that you miss. You build one by comparing the link lists of two or three rivals, and you mark the domains that appear on every list. You start your outreach with those shared domains, because they already link to sites like yours.

How many competitor backlinks should I copy?

You copy the best twenty links first, and you work through them one at a time with a real pitch. You never copy hundreds at once, because a rushed pitch wins nothing. You add new links to the list as you win the first ones, and the list grows with your wins.

Should I copy links from any competitor?

You copy links only from competitors in your niche, because a link from your topic helps more than a random link. You skip rivals that buy links or run spam networks, and you never copy their tactics. You build your list from honest competitors, and your links stay safe.

How do I learn to find competitor backlinks as a beginner?

You learn how to find competitor backlinks for beginners by running a free checker on your strongest rival and reading the list it returns. You mark the shared links, sort them by relevance, and pitch the best twenty one at a time. You then track the replies, and you add new rivals to the list as you grow.
