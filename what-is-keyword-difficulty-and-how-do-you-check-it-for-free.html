What Is Keyword Difficulty and How Do You Check It for Free?

What is keyword difficulty? A score that shows how hard a keyword is to win. Learn how tools build it, how to read the top ten and how to check it free.

You can avoid unwinnable keywords with one simple score. The score tells you how hard a keyword is to rank for. This guide explains what is keyword difficulty and how you can check it for free.

Most beginners target broad keywords with high scores and wonder why nothing ranks. Their pages sit on page ten while strong sites own the top ten. They learn slowly that a hard keyword wastes months of effort.

After this guide you will read a difficulty score with care. You will check the top ten pages before you trust it. You will pick keywords your site can win.

Keyword Difficulty Scores How Hard a Keyword Is to Win

You read keyword difficulty as a score from zero to one hundred. A low score means few strong pages compete for that keyword. A high score means strong pages with many links own the top ten. You learn what is keyword difficulty by comparing scores for keywords you know.

You use the score as a first filter, not as a final verdict. You compare the score with your own site strength. You pick keywords with scores your site can beat. You skip keywords that only giant brands can win.

A new blog checks the score for best laptops and it sees a score of ninety. It checks best laptops for students under 500 dollars and it sees a score of thirty. The blog picks the lower score and it ranks within three months. You filter your keywords the same way.

Each Tool Builds Its Score in Its Own Way

You learn that each tool builds its difficulty score in its own way. Ahrefs counts the links of the top ten pages, Semrush blends links with content quality and search features, and Moz counts the authority of the top ten domains. You read each score with the tool method in mind.

You compare scores across tools before you trust one number. You run the same keyword through two free tools. You note where the tools agree and where they differ. You treat a big gap as a sign to check the top ten yourself.

A marketer checks one keyword in Ahrefs and Semrush. Ahrefs shows a score of forty while Semrush shows a score of sixty, so she opens the top ten pages and she finds weak pages with few links. She targets the keyword because the pages look beatable. You check two tools the same way before you decide.

Read the Top Ten Pages Before You Trust a Score

You open the top ten pages for a keyword before you trust any score. You check whether the pages are strong brands or weak blogs. You count the links on each page with a free tool. You learn what is keyword difficulty by reading real pages, not by trusting a number alone.

You look for weak pages in the top ten, such as old posts with thin content. You check whether the pages answer the search intent well. You pick keywords where at least two top ten pages look weak. You skip keywords where every top ten page looks strong.

A beginner checks the top ten for a local keyword and she finds three weak pages. Two pages have no images and one page has no clear answer. She writes a better page and she ranks in the top five. You read the top ten the same way and you pick beatable keywords.

New Sites Win by Picking Lower Scores

You pick lower difficulty keywords when your site is new and weak. You target scores under thirty while your site builds links. You write the best page for each keyword you pick. You learn what is keyword difficulty by winning small keywords first.

You grow your site strength with each small win. You earn links from useful pages and your authority rises. You target harder keywords only after you win easier ones. You keep a sheet of keywords by score and you move up slowly.

A new site targets twenty keywords with scores under twenty five. It ranks for fifteen of them within six months. It then targets keywords with scores near forty. You start with low scores the same way and you climb step by step.

Re Check Difficulty as Your Site Grows

You re check difficulty as your site grows stronger. You review your keyword list once a quarter. You move keywords up the list when your site earns more links. You drop keywords that grow too hard because rivals improved.

You track the scores of your target keywords in a simple sheet. You note the score and the date each time you check. You compare the scores over time to see the trend. You keep your list fresh so you always target winnable keywords.

An agency reviews its keyword list each quarter and it finds a shift. Five keywords grew harder because rivals published better pages. The agency drops those keywords and it picks five fresh ones. You review your list the same way and you keep it current.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is keyword difficulty in simple words?

Keyword difficulty is a score from zero to one hundred that shows how hard a keyword is to rank for. A low score means weak competition. A high score means strong pages own the top ten.

How do I check keyword difficulty for free?

You can check difficulty with free tools such as Ahrefs Webmaster Tools and Ubersuggest. You can also read the top ten pages yourself. You should compare two tools before you trust one score.

What is a good keyword difficulty score?

A good score depends on your site strength. New sites should target scores under thirty. Strong sites can target scores up to sixty or more.

Is keyword difficulty the same in every tool?

No, each tool builds its score in its own way. Ahrefs counts links while Semrush blends links and content. You should compare two tools and read the top ten pages.

Can a new site rank for a hard keyword?

A new site rarely ranks for a hard keyword because strong pages own the top ten. You should win low difficulty keywords first. You can target harder keywords as your site grows.

Should I only target low difficulty keywords?

No, you should mix low difficulty keywords with a few medium ones. You can win low scores fast and build strength for harder ones. You should keep your list balanced each quarter.

How often should I check keyword difficulty?

You should check difficulty when you pick a new keyword. You should review your list once a quarter. You should drop keywords that grow too hard.

Does keyword difficulty include search volume?

No, difficulty and volume are separate numbers. A keyword can have high volume and high difficulty. You should check both numbers before you pick a keyword.
