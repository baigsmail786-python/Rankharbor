7 Common Link-Building Mistakes That Waste Budget (and How to Avoid Them)

Buying on metrics alone, ignoring relevance, over-optimized anchors and more — the seven link-building mistakes we see most often, and the fix for each.

Mistake 1: Buying on a single metric

Choosing placements by one authority score invites manipulation — the score can be inflated while the site is worthless. Fix: evaluate relevance, audience, editorial standards and link patterns with a proper checklist.

Mistake 2: Ignoring topical relevance

A link from an unrelated site helps little and can look manipulative at scale. Fix: prioritize sites your buyers read; relevance beats raw authority in most SaaS and B2B niches.

Mistake 3: Over-optimized anchor text

If every link uses your exact money keyword, the pattern is unnatural. Fix: use a natural mix — branded, topical, partial-match and plain URL anchors.

Mistake 4: Linking only to the homepage

Deep pages — product, feature and strong content — need links to rank. Fix: distribute links across the pages mapped to your keyword clusters.

Mistake 5: Volume over quality

Fifty weak links rarely beat five strong ones, and they add risk. Fix: set a quality bar (our nine-point evaluation) and let volume follow from it.

Mistake 6: No verification or reporting

Paying for placements you never verify is common and avoidable. Fix: require live URLs, placement screenshots and a log of anchor text and target pages for every link.

Mistake 7: Expecting instant rankings

Links take time to be crawled, weighted and reflected in results. Fix: judge campaigns over a quarter, track leading indicators (indexation, referring-domain quality, movement on target clusters) and keep expectations honest — no reputable provider guarantees rankings.
