What Are Editorial Backlinks and How Do You Earn Them?

Learn what editorial backlinks are, how they differ from guest post links, and how original research, mentions, and linkable assets earn them.

An editorial backlink is a link that a writer adds because your page deserves a mention. No payment changes hands and no pitch forces the link. This guide explains what are editorial backlinks and how to earn them in plain words.

Most site owners chase links with mass emails while they publish pages no writer would cite. They ask for links before they earn them. This habit fills inboxes and earns few links.

You will learn what makes a link editorial, which content earns it, and how to win mentions from writers who trust your work.

What Editorial Backlinks Mean In SEO

An editorial backlink is a link that an editor or writer places in a page because the linked page adds value. The writer chooses the link freely after reading your page and finding it useful. Google trusts these links because no money and no deal shaped the choice.

Editorial links carry weight because they signal real trust from a publisher. A writer stakes their reputation on every link they add to an article. Google reads that choice as a vote that your page holds words worth citing.

Hassan runs a fishing gear shop in Sialkot and he wrote a guide about river fishing knots. A fishing magazine writer read the guide and linked it inside an article about beginner gear. Hassan never pitched the magazine, yet the link arrived because his guide answered the question better than rival pages.

How Editorial Links Differ From Guest Post Links

A guest post link comes from an article that you write and place on another site. An editorial link comes from an article that another writer writes and links to your page on their own. The difference sits in who writes the article and who chooses the link.

Google treats the two links differently because the editorial link shows unprompted trust. A guest post link shows that you placed the content yourself, so it carries less weight as a vote. You learn what are editorial backlinks and how to earn them by building pages that writers cite without a deal.

Fatima owns a skincare clinic in Peshawar and she paid for guest posts that brought weak links. She then published a guide with before and after photos from her own clinic. Beauty writers linked the guide inside their own articles, and those editorial links lifted her rankings faster than the guest posts ever did.

How Original Research Earns Editorial Links

Original research earns editorial links because no other page holds the same findings. You survey your customers, study your own data, or test a claim that writers repeat without proof. The findings give writers a fresh source that only your page can offer.

You keep the study simple so writers trust the numbers and quote them with ease. You show the method in plain words and you present the findings in short lines that fit a quote. You update the study each year so writers keep citing the fresh version.

Ali runs a bike repair shop in Gujranwala and he tracked which brake pads lasted longest on city roads. He published the results with clear notes on how he tested each brand. Cycling blogs linked his study because it answered a question that no local page had tested with real data.

How Unlinked Mentions Turn Into Editorial Links

An unlinked mention happens when a writer names your brand without adding a link to your site. The writer already trusts your brand enough to name it, so the step to a link stays small. You find these mentions by searching your brand name and reading each result.

You turn a mention into a link with a short polite note to the writer. You thank the writer for the mention and you point to the page that fits the mention best. You ask for nothing else, and most writers add the link because it helps their own readers.

Sana owns a catering firm in Hyderabad and a food blog named her firm in a wedding guide without a link. She sent the writer a short note with a link to her menu page. The writer added the link the same week, and her site gained a strong editorial link from a page that already trusted her brand.

How Linkable Assets Keep Earning Editorial Links

A linkable asset is a page that keeps earning links because it solves a problem that writers cannot solve with words alone. A calculator, a checklist, a template, or a clear guide gives readers a thing they can use at once. Writers link the asset because it helps their own readers finish a task.

You build the asset around one clear job and you keep it free to use without a signup wall. You add a short guide beside the asset so readers learn the job and the tool together. You refresh the asset when the job changes so writers keep linking the live version.

Kamran runs a tax consultancy in Quetta and he built a simple tax calculator for small shop owners. Business blogs linked the calculator inside guides about filing taxes. Frequently Asked Questions

What is an editorial backlink?

An editorial backlink is a link that a writer adds to your page because it strengthens their article. You learn what are editorial backlinks and how to earn them by publishing pages that writers cite without payment or a deal. Google trusts these links because no money shaped the choice.

How is an editorial link different from a guest post link?

An editorial link comes from an article that another writer writes and links to your page on their own. A guest post link comes from an article that you write and place on another site. The editorial link carries more weight because it shows unprompted trust.

Do editorial links help rankings more than other links?

Editorial links help rankings more because they come from publishers that chose your page for its value. Google reads the choice as a real vote from a trusted source. One editorial link from a respected site often beats many weak links from low quality pages.

Can I ask a writer for an editorial link?

You can show a writer your page and explain the fresh value it holds, yet you should never demand a link in return. You send a short note that names the value and you let the writer decide. Writers link pages they trust, so a polite note beats a pushy ask every time.

What content earns editorial links fastest?

Original research, free tools, and clear guides earn editorial links fastest because writers cannot find the same value elsewhere. A page with fresh data gives a writer a source that only you can offer. You pick the format that fits the data or the skill your team already holds.

How do I find unlinked mentions of my brand?

You search your brand name in Google and you set up alerts that mail you each new mention. You read each result and you note the pages that name your brand without a link. You then send the writer a short note that points to the page that fits the mention best.

Do small websites earn editorial links?

A small website earns editorial links when one strong page gives writers something they cannot find elsewhere. You need no large budget when you study your own customers or test a claim with real users. You start with one small study and you let the links grow as writers find it.

How long does it take to earn editorial links?

Editorial links arrive slowly because writers must find your page and trust its value before they cite it. Most pages gather their first editorial links within a few weeks of a good launch. You speed the wait by showing the page to writers who already cite similar pages.
