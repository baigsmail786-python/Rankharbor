Learn What a Content Brief Does for a Writer

You write a content brief so a writer knows the goal of the page before they type one word. The brief names the reader and the keyword and the outcome that the page must deliver. You give the writer one clear target and the writer stops guessing what you want.

You treat the brief as the plan for the page and not as extra paperwork. A good brief saves you the hours that a rewrite costs and it saves the writer the doubt that a vague order brings. You spend twenty minutes on the brief and you save hours on the draft.

Add the Keyword and the Search Intent

You place the primary keyword at the top of the brief so the writer sees it before they write. You write the search intent in plain words beside the keyword and you name what the reader wants to learn. You add the related phrases that the writer must use with care.

You show the writer how the keyword must appear in the title and the first lines and the headings. You tell the writer to use the keyword with care and never to force it into a sentence. You add the questions that the reader asks so the writer answers them in the body.

What is the difference between a content brief and a creative brief?

A content brief tells a writer what to write for one page and it names the keyword and the reader need. A creative brief tells a design team the look and the feel of a whole campaign. You use a content brief when you order a page for search and you use a creative brief when you order a design or an ad. You keep the two documents apart so each team gets the orders that fit its work.
