How Do Beginners Use Ubersuggest to Find Keywords for Free?

Learn how beginners use Ubersuggest to find keywords for free. See keyword ideas, search volume, and difficulty scores, and pick targets that fit a new site.

Short answer: Ubersuggest is a keyword tool that shows keyword ideas, search volume, and a difficulty score for any phrase you type. You type a seed word and read the Keyword Ideas report. You learn how to use Ubersuggest for keyword research for beginners by turning that report into a short list of targets.

Most beginners type one broad word, grab the first idea, and write a page around it. They skip the volume and difficulty columns and pick a phrase their new site cannot win. The tool showed the warning signs, yet they never read them.

You finish able to find free keywords and pick winners.

Open Ubersuggest and Run Your First Search

You start how to use Ubersuggest for keyword research for beginners by opening the tool and typing one seed word. A seed word is a short phrase from your niche, such as dentist, bakery, or plumbing. The tool reads that word and builds a list of related ideas in seconds. That first search turns a blank page into a plan you can work with.

Type a Seed Word From Your Niche

Pick a seed word that names the service or topic your site covers, not a broad word that covers the whole internet. A dentist in Lahore should type dentist Lahore, while a bakery should type chocolate cake order. The seed word shapes every idea the tool returns, so a sharp seed word gives a sharp list. A vague seed word gives a vague list that wastes your hour.

Read the Overview Before You Open the Ideas

The overview page shows the search volume, the difficulty score, and the paid difficulty for your seed word at a glance. Read those three numbers first, because they tell you whether the seed word deserves a page of its own. A seed word with huge volume and a high difficulty score needs a different plan from a seed word with modest volume and a low score. That quick read stops you from chasing a phrase that your site cannot win yet.

Read the Keyword Ideas Report Like a Researcher

The Keyword Ideas report holds the real value of Ubersuggest, so open it and read it like a researcher. The report lists related phrases, questions, and prepositions that real people type around your seed word. Each row shows volume, difficulty, and paid difficulty beside the phrase. Those columns turn a list of words into a set of choices you can judge.

Sort Ideas by Volume and Difficulty Together

Sorting by volume alone leads you to hard phrases, while sorting by difficulty alone leads you to phrases nobody types. Sort by both columns together and look for rows where the volume reads fair and the difficulty reads low. A Lahore plumber who sorts this way finds emergency plumber in Lahore with steady demand and weak rivals. That row becomes a page that can rank and bring calls.

Use Question Ideas to Find Blog Topics

The report also lists the questions people ask around your seed word, and those questions make strong blog topics. A question shows the exact words a reader types when confusion strikes, so the answer you write matches the search. A page that answers one clear question earns trust and often earns the click. Use the Difficulty Score to Pick Winners

The difficulty score inside Ubersuggest tells you how hard each idea will be to win, so use it as your filter. The tool names the score SEO difficulty, and it runs on the same idea as every other tool. A low score means weak rivals, while a high score means strong rivals. You learn how to use Ubersuggest for keyword research for beginners when you let that score shape every pick.

Aim New Pages at Low Difficulty Ideas

A new site should aim its first pages at ideas with low difficulty scores and clear intent. Those pages rank sooner, bring the first visits, and teach you which topics your readers love. A page that targets a hard idea sits unseen for months and drains your drive. Start low, win early, and climb to harder ideas as your site earns links.

Compare the Free and Paid Views of the Same Data

Ubersuggest shows a free view of its keyword data and a fuller paid view for subscribers, so learn what each view gives you. The free view shows keyword ideas, volume, and the difficulty score for your searches. The paid view adds deeper reports and more history for teams that research all day. Turn Free Keyword Ideas Into Published Pages

Keyword ideas earn nothing until they become pages, so turn your short list into published articles. Pick one idea per page and match the page type to the intent behind the phrase. A question idea becomes a guide, while a buying idea becomes a comparison or a service page. You learn how to use Ubersuggest for keyword research for beginners when your research ends in pages that readers can open.

Write One Page for Each Keyword Idea

One idea deserves one page, because a page that chases five ideas serves none of them well. A page about emergency plumber in Lahore should answer that search alone, with local detail and a clear call to the plumber. Readers find the answer fast, and Google reads the focus of the page with ease. That focus beats a broad page that mentions the phrase once and moves on.

Track Which Pages Earn Visits and Repeat the Win

Publishing ends the research, yet tracking starts the learning, so watch which pages earn visits in Search Console. A page that earns clicks tells you the idea worked, and a page that earns none tells you to fix the title or the content. Repeat the winning pattern with fresh ideas from the same report. That loop turns one free search into a steady stream of pages that grow the site.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ubersuggest free for keyword research?

Ubersuggest offers a free view that shows keyword ideas, search volume, and a difficulty score for your searches. The free view covers the basics a beginner needs to pick targets. A paid plan adds deeper reports for teams that research all day.

How do I start a keyword search in Ubersuggest?

Open Ubersuggest, type a seed word from your niche, and read the Keyword Ideas report. The report lists related phrases with volume and difficulty beside each one. Anyone who learns how to use Ubersuggest for keyword research for beginners starts with that one search.

What is the Keyword Ideas report in Ubersuggest?

The Keyword Ideas report lists related phrases, questions, and prepositions around a seed word. Each row shows search volume, SEO difficulty, and paid difficulty for the phrase. Writers use the report to build a short list of targets.

What does the SEO difficulty score mean in Ubersuggest?

The SEO difficulty score shows how hard a new page will find it to rank for a phrase. A low score points to weak rivals, while a high score points to strong rivals. Read the score beside the intent before you pick a target.

Can beginners rely on Ubersuggest alone for keyword research?

Beginners can start with Ubersuggest alone, yet a second free source sharpens the plan. Google autocomplete and Search Console show real searches from real people. Pair the tool with those sources for a fuller picture.

How many keywords should a new site target first?

A new site should target a short list of low difficulty ideas with clear intent, roughly ten pages to start. Ten focused pages teach you more than fifty vague ones. Grow the list as the first pages earn visits.

Does Ubersuggest show search volume for every keyword?

Ubersuggest shows a volume figure for most keywords in its database, yet some rare phrases show no data at all. A phrase with no volume data may still bring visits when it matches a real question. Judge such phrases by the top results, not by the blank column.

Should I pay for Ubersuggest as a beginner?

A beginner should learn the free view first and pay only when the free view limits real work. The free view covers keyword ideas, volume, and difficulty for early research. Spend money on the paid plan when your site grows past those basics.
