Domain Authority vs Domain Rating: What Is the Difference and Which One Matters?

Domain authority and domain rating both predict ranking strength, and they measure different things. Learn what each score means, why the numbers disagree, and which one deserves your attention.

Short answer: Domain authority is a Moz score from 1 to 100, and domain rating is an Ahrefs score from 0 to 100. Both scores predict how well a site may rank, and both come from link data rather than from Google. Domain authority vs domain rating differs in the math behind each number.

Many beginners place the two numbers side by side and read them as one scale. The scales share a 1 to 100 range, and they measure different things with different link data. A direct comparison misleads the reader.

You will learn what each score measures, why the numbers disagree, and which score deserves your attention.

What Domain Authority Measures

Moz built domain authority to predict ranking strength across many sites, and Moz calculates it from link data and other signals. The score runs from 1 to 100, and new sites start near the bottom of that range. Strong sites with many trusted links sit near the top, and the score rises slowly as the link profile grows.

A local bakery site with 20 links from local blogs may hold a score near 15. A national news site with links from thousands of trusted sites may hold a score near 90. The gap shows link strength, and it also shows age and size. You read the number as a rough guide to link strength, and you never read it as a Google rank.

One link from a respected university counts for more than 50 links from thin directory pages, and depth on one subject builds the same kind of trust. Moz updates the score as its link index changes, so your number moves when your link profile changes. You track the trend across months, and you ignore small moves from week to week. The steps that lift the score appear in What Is Domain Authority in SEO and How Do You Improve It?.

What Domain Rating Measures

Ahrefs built domain rating to measure the strength of a site link profile, and Ahrefs calculates it from the quality of the domains that link to the site. The score runs from 0 to 100, and it rises when strong domains link to the site. A site with links from a few powerful domains can outscore a site with links from hundreds of weak domains.

Domain rating weighs the linking domain rather than the raw link count, and that choice sets it apart from domain authority. A link from a strong news site lifts the score more than 100 links from new blogs. The score counts each linking domain once, so one domain gives one vote no matter how many pages it links from.

A small tool site earned links from three respected software blogs, and its domain rating jumped past sites with far more links. The three blogs held high scores of their own, and their links passed real strength to the small site. The example shows why one strong link beats a pile of weak ones, and why the raw count misleads.

Why the Two Scores Disagree on the Same Site

Domain authority vs domain rating splits beginners because the two scores disagree on the same site, and the split fades once you see the two causes. The scores read different link indexes and apply different math to the links they find. Moz and Ahrefs crawl the web on their own schedules, and each one finds a different set of links to your site. The same site can hold a domain authority of 35 and a domain rating of 52 on the same day, and neither number is wrong.

Different Link Indexes

Moz finds one set of links, Ahrefs finds another set, and neither crawler sees the whole web. A link that Moz missed still lifts your Ahrefs score, and a link that Ahrefs missed still lifts your Moz score. The gap between the two numbers often shows the gap between the two indexes rather than a change in your site. You treat each score as a view from one tool, and you never mix the two views into one number.

Different Math Behind Each Number

Domain authority blends link counts with link quality and other signals into one number. Domain rating weighs the strength of the linking domains and the number of unique domains. A site with many links from weak domains can hold a fair domain authority and a low domain rating at the same time. The math explains the gap, and the gap tells you which kind of links you lack. Deep coverage of one subject earns the strong links that lift both scores, and you can learn that method in What Is Topical Authority in SEO and How Do You Build It?.

Which Score Matters for Your Work

When you weigh domain authority vs domain rating for your own tracking, one score matters as a tool and neither matters as a target. You pick the tool that your team already uses, and you track that one score each month. A rising trend shows that your links grow stronger, and a flat trend shows that your link work needs fresh effort.

You compare your score with direct rivals inside the same tool, and you set your target above theirs. A score of 25 can lead a small niche when every rival sits below 20, and a score of 40 can lag in a crowded niche when rivals sit above 60. The rival gap decides your target, and the tool you pick decides the number you watch.

You also pair the score with traffic, leads, and ranking pages before you judge any progress. A rising score with no new ranking pages points to links that bring no traffic. A flat score with new ranking pages shows that your content does the heavy work, and the score follows the content in time.

A Simple Way to Use Both Scores

Domain authority vs domain rating is a choice between two views of the same link profile, and you need only one view for daily work. You pick one tool, track its score each month, and compare it with rivals inside that same tool. The number guides your link work, and it never replaces traffic and leads as the real proof of progress.

You start this month by recording both scores next to your link count and your ranking pages. You earn links from real sites, fix weak pages, and publish guides that help readers. You check both scores next month, and you keep going when the trend points up.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between domain authority and domain rating?

Domain authority is a Moz score from 1 to 100, and domain rating is an Ahrefs score from 0 to 100. Domain authority blends link counts with link quality, and domain rating weighs the strength of the linking domains. Both scores predict ranking strength from link data, and neither score comes from Google.

Is domain authority or domain rating more accurate?

Neither score is accurate in an absolute sense, because each one reads a different link index with different math. You pick one tool and compare your score with rivals inside that same tool. The trend inside one tool tells you more than any single number from either tool.

Does Google use domain authority or domain rating?

No. Google never uses either score in ranking, and both scores come from third party tools. Google ranks pages on its own signals, and strong links help both the scores and the ranks. A high score often sits next to a strong rank because strong links drive both.

Can a site have high domain authority and low domain rating?

Yes. A site with many links from weak domains can hold a fair domain authority and a low domain rating at the same time. Domain rating weighs the strength of linking domains, so weak links lift it less. The gap points to the kind of links you lack, and strong links close it over time.

What is a good domain rating score?

A good score depends on your niche and your rivals. A domain rating of 30 can lead a small niche when rivals sit below 25. A domain rating of 50 can lag in a crowded niche when rivals sit above 70. You compare your score with direct rivals inside Ahrefs, and you set your target above theirs.

How do I check domain authority and domain rating for free?

Moz offers a free checker for domain authority, and Ahrefs offers free tools that show domain rating for a site. You check one domain in each tool, and you record both numbers next to the date. You compare scores only inside the same tool, and you track the trend each month.

Which score should beginners track?

Beginners should track the score from the tool they already use. If you use Ahrefs, track domain rating each month. If you use Moz, track domain authority each month. One score tracked each month beats two scores checked at random, and the trend shows whether your link work pays off.

Do backlinks raise both domain authority and domain rating?

Yes. Links from trusted sites lift both scores over time, because both scores read link data. One link from a respected site counts for more than many links from thin pages in both systems. Links that bring real readers also help your traffic, and that double gain makes them the links to chase.
