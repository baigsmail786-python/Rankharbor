What Is Domain Authority in SEO and Should Beginners Care About It?

Learn what is domain authority in SEO for beginners, how Moz builds the 1 to 100 score, and a 90 day plan to lift it step by step for a new website.

What Domain Authority Is and How the Score Works

Domain authority is a third party score from Moz that predicts how well a site can rank in search results. The score runs from 1 to 100, and a new site starts near the bottom while large trusted sites sit near the top. Moz builds the score from link data, and it weighs how many sites link to you and how trusted they look. You treat the score as a compass, not a prize, because Google never uses it in its own ranking system.

Where the Score Comes From

Moz studies the links that point to your site and the links that point to every other site on the web. It counts linking domains rather than single links, so ten links from ten sites beat ten links from one site. It also weighs the trust of each linking site, so a link from a respected news site counts far more than a link from a fresh directory. You raise the score by earning links from trusted sites, not by collecting links from anywhere.

What Counts as a Good Score

A good score depends on your rivals, and a score of 30 can win in a quiet niche while a score of 50 can lose in a crowded one. New sites often sit below 10, and that low start is normal for the first months of work. You compare your score with the sites that rank for your main keywords, and you learn what is domain authority in SEO for beginners by watching how the gap closes as you earn links. A bakery site in Faisalabad that reaches 25 can outrank local rivals near 15, because the gap in trust is real.

How to Check Your Domain Authority for Free

You check your score with a free tool, and you read the number beside the numbers that explain it. Moz Link Explorer gives a free look at the score, and it shows linking domains and spam score beside it. You run the check once a month, and you write the number down so you can see the trend. You never judge a site by one check alone, because the score moves slowly and small jumps mean little on their own.

Use a Free Checker the Right Way

You open Moz Link Explorer, paste your home page address, and read the domain authority number at the top of the report. You then scroll to the linking domains count, because that count drives the score more than any other single number. You also read the spam score, and a high spam score warns you that bad links may hold your site back. You save the three numbers in a simple sheet, and you repeat the same check on the same day each month.

Read Your Score Beside Your Rivals

You list the five sites that rank above you for your main keyword, and you check each one with the same free tool. You write their scores beside yours, and you look for the gap between your site and the lowest scorer on page one. That gap tells you how much link work you need, and it keeps your goal honest. A pet grooming site that sees rivals at 20 knows it needs steady links, while a site that sees rivals at 60 must win a different way first.

How Beginners Raise Domain Authority the Honest Way

You raise domain authority by earning links that Google trusts, and you earn those links with pages worth linking to. You publish one strong guide that beats the pages that rank now. You then tell the right people about it, and you ask for a link in plain words. The work is slow, and each good link lifts the score a little, so you repeat the cycle every month.

Earn Links from Pages That Relate to Your Topic

A link helps most when the linking page covers your topic, because Google reads the match between the two pages. You find blogs and guides in your niche, and you offer them a page their readers need. You write a short email that names their page, points to your guide, and explains the gain for their readers. Fix the Weak Spots on Your Own Site

Links from other sites help only when your own site holds together, so you fix the weak spots first. You remove broken pages, speed up slow pages, and link your own pages together so Google can crawl them all. You also check for spammy links that point to you, and you ask Google to ignore the worst ones through the disavow tool. A small travel blog fixed its broken links and saw its score climb within two months, because Google could finally crawl the site cleanly.

A new site needs a plan of small weekly jobs, and this plan gives you one job per week. You build the base in the first month, and you earn links in the next two months. You check your score at the end of each month, and you keep the jobs that move the number. You finish the 90 days with real links, a clean site, and a score that reflects both.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is domain authority?

Domain authority is a score from 1 to 100 that Moz created to predict how well a website can rank on Google. It rests on link data, and it weighs how many sites link to you and how trusted those sites look. Google never uses the score in its own ranking, so you treat it as a guide for comparing sites.

Is domain authority a Google ranking factor?

Domain authority is not a Google ranking factor, because Google built its own systems and never adopted the Moz score. Google reads links, content, and user signals directly, and the Moz score only predicts the result of those signals. You still track it, because it shows how your link profile compares with your rivals.

What is a good domain authority for a new website?

A new website often starts with a score below 10, and that low start is normal in the first months. A good target sits a few points above the weakest rival on page one for your main keyword. You set that target after you check five rivals, and you raise it as your links grow.

How long does it take to raise domain authority?

Domain authority moves slowly, and most new sites see the first clear move after two to three months of steady link work. Each trusted link lifts the score a little, and the score climbs faster once your site passes the low teens. You measure progress monthly, and you judge the trend over a quarter rather than a single week.

Can I buy links to raise my domain authority fast?

Buying links can push the score up for a short time, and it can also bring a penalty that drops your rankings hard. Google reads paid links as a trick, and it can remove your pages from the results when it finds them. You earn links with strong pages and honest outreach, and that path keeps your rankings safe.

Does domain authority differ from domain rating?

Domain authority comes from Moz, and domain rating comes from Ahrefs, so the two scores use different data and different maths. A site can hold a domain authority of 30 and a domain rating of 45 at the same time, and neither number is wrong. You pick one tool, track it monthly, and compare your site with rivals on that same scale.

How do I check domain authority for free?

You check domain authority for free with Moz Link Explorer, and you get a limited number of free checks each month. You paste your home page address, read the score, and note the linking domains and spam score beside it. You repeat the check monthly, and you record each result so the trend stays clear.

Does domain authority matter for local businesses?

Domain authority matters less for a local business than reviews, a complete profile, and pages that name the town. A local bakery wins map results with strong reviews even when its score sits near 15. You still build links over time, and the score grows as a side effect of that work.
