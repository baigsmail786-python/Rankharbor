How Do Beginners Use Internal Links to Help Google Find Their Pages?

Learn how to use internal links for SEO for beginners, pick anchor text that helps Google, and run a one hour audit that finds orphan pages.

Short answer: Internal links join one page on your site to another page on the same site, and they help Google find, crawl, and rank your pages. You learn how to use internal links for SEO for beginners by linking new pages from strong pages and fixing orphan pages.

Most beginners publish new pages and never link to them from anywhere. Those orphan pages sit alone, so Google crawls them late and ranks them low.

You will link every new page from your homepage, pick clear anchor text, and run a one hour audit. You will finish with every page a few clicks from the homepage.

What Internal Links Do for Google and Readers

Internal links are the paths that join your pages together, and they guide both Google and your readers. Google follows them to find new pages, and it reads them to learn which pages relate to each other. Readers follow them to go deeper on a topic, and they stay on your site longer when the next step is clear. You build these paths with care, and your whole site works better for it.

How Links Help Google Crawl Your Site

Google finds new pages by following links from pages it already knows, and your homepage is the first page it checks. A new guide with no links in has no path for Google to follow, so it may sit unseen for weeks. You link each new page from the homepage or from a related guide, and Google finds it on the next crawl. A small shop blog that links every new post from its homepage sees new pages appear in search within days.

How Links Pass Value Between Pages

Links pass value from strong pages to the pages they point to, and that value helps the linked page rank. Your homepage usually holds the most value, because other sites link to it most. You point that value at the pages you want to rank by linking to them from the homepage and from your best guides. You learn how to use internal links for SEO for beginners by pointing strong pages at the pages that need a lift, and you watch those pages climb.

How to Place Internal Links That Work

You place internal links where they help the reader, and you write them so Google reads the topic clearly. You add links in the body of the page, near the words that relate to the linked page. You use anchor text that names the topic, and you avoid vague words like click here. You add two or three links per page, and you link to pages that truly relate to the text around them.

Link New Pages from Strong Pages

You link each new page from your strongest related page, and you do it on the day you publish. You find the guide that already ranks or earns the most visits, and you add a link to the new page in a fitting paragraph. You also link the new page back to the strong page when the topics connect both ways. A fitness blog that links a new stretching guide from its popular workout plan page gives the new guide a fast start.

Write Anchor Text That Names the Topic

Anchor text is the clickable words of a link, and it tells Google what the linked page covers. You write anchor text that names the topic in plain words, and you match it to the title of the page you link to. You vary the anchor text across your site, and you never use the same phrase for two different pages. A link that reads best free keyword research tools tells Google far more than a link that reads click here.

How to Find and Fix Orphan Pages

Orphan pages are pages with no internal links pointing to them, and they hide from Google and from readers. You find them with a crawl, and you fix them by adding links from fitting pages. You check for orphans every month, because each new batch of pages can leave one behind. You treat an orphan page as a page that does not exist yet, and you link it in at once.

Spot Pages with No Links In

You spot orphan pages with a free crawl tool, and you look for pages that show zero links in. You run a crawl of your whole site, and you sort the pages by the number of links that point to them. Fix Orphans with Three Simple Moves

You fix an orphan with three moves, and you start with the move that fits best. You link it from the homepage when the page matters to the whole site, and you link it from a related guide when the topic fits one page well. You add it to a category or hub page when it belongs with a group of pages on the same topic. A recipe site once fixed its orphan recipes by linking them from its main category pages, and those recipes started to rank within a month.

A One Hour Internal Link Audit for Beginners

You can audit your internal links in one hour with free tools, and this checklist splits the hour into two parts. You spend 15 minutes on the crawl and the list, and you spend 45 minutes on the fixes. You finish the hour with a clear list of fixed pages and a routine you can repeat monthly. You run the audit on the same day each month, and your site stays free of orphans.

The 15 Minute Crawl and List

You open a free crawl tool, enter your site address, and let it crawl every page it can reach. You export the list of pages, and you sort it by the count of internal links each page receives. You write down every page with zero links in, and you add every page that sits more than three clicks from the homepage. You also note pages with only one link in, because those pages hang by a thread.

The 45 Minute Fix List

You work through your list from the top, and you fix the pages that matter most first. You add a link to each orphan from the most fitting guide, and you write anchor text that names its topic. You add links to deep pages from the homepage or from a hub page, and you shorten their path to two clicks. Frequently Asked Questions

What is an internal link?

An internal link is a link from one page on your site to another page on the same site. It helps Google find and crawl your pages, and it helps readers move to the next useful page. Every page on your site should hold at least one internal link in.

How many internal links should a page have?

A page should hold two or three internal links to pages that truly relate to its topic. You add links where they help the reader, and you skip links that only pad the count. A long guide can hold more links, and a short page can hold fewer, so you let the content decide.

What is anchor text in internal linking?

Anchor text is the clickable text of a link, and it tells Google and the reader what the linked page covers. You write anchor text that names the topic in plain words, and you match it to the linked page title. You avoid vague words like click here, because they tell Google nothing.

What is an orphan page in SEO?

An orphan page is a page that no other page on your site links to, so Google and readers struggle to find it. It may still rank when an outside site links to it, and it usually ranks low. You find orphans with a crawl, and you fix them by linking them in from fitting pages.

Do internal links help rankings?

Internal links help rankings, because they pass value from strong pages to the pages they point to. They also help Google crawl new pages fast, and they show Google how your pages relate. A site with clean internal links ranks better than a site where pages sit alone.

Should I link to my homepage from every page?

You do not need to link to your homepage from every page, because your navigation already links to it. You link to the pages that need the lift, and you let the navigation handle the homepage. You use body links for pages that deserve extra attention from Google and readers.

Can too many internal links hurt a site?

Too many internal links can hurt a site when they fill a page with links that add no value. Google reads a page stuffed with links as spam, and readers find it hard to use. You keep links few and fitting, and you add a link only when it helps the reader take a useful next step.

How often should I audit my internal links?

You audit your internal links once a month, and you check for orphans and deep pages each time. A monthly audit takes about an hour, and it keeps your site clean as you publish new pages. You run it on the same day each month, and you fix what you find before you publish more.
