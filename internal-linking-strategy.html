How to Create an Effective Internal Linking Strategy

Internal links distribute authority, guide crawlers and help readers. Build a strategy with hubs, contextual links and anchor discipline — and audit it quarterly.

Why internal linking punches above its weight

Internal links are the one ranking lever fully in your control. They tell search engines which pages matter, distribute authority from strong pages to strategic ones, and help crawlers discover content. Most sites underuse them badly.

Step 1: Define your hub structure

Organize content into topic hubs: a pillar page per cluster, supported by related articles that all link back to the pillar and across to siblings where genuinely relevant. Your keyword map (clusters and target pages) is the blueprint.

Step 2: Link contextually, with intent

The highest-value internal links sit inside body copy, in sentences where the linked page genuinely helps the reader continue. Link from high-authority pages (homepage, popular guides) toward priority commercial pages — many sites discover their pricing page has almost no internal support.

Step 3: Use descriptive, varied anchors

Anchor text should describe the destination honestly. Vary phrasing naturally; repeating one exact-match anchor hundreds of times looks engineered and helps no one. Avoid 'click here' — it wastes the signal and hurts accessibility.

Step 4: Fix the failures

Audit quarterly for:

  • Orphan pages with zero internal links
  • Broken internal links and links to redirected URLs (update to final destinations)
  • Important pages buried more than 3–4 clicks deep
  • Over-linked boilerplate that dilutes contextual signals

Step 5: Make it a habit, not a project

Add internal-link review to your publishing checklist: every new article links to its pillar and 2–3 siblings, and gets linked from at least two existing pages at launch. Our on-page SEO service builds this into monthly campaigns so the structure stays healthy as you publish.
