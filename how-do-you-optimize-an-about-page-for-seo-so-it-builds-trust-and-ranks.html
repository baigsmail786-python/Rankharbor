How Do You Optimize an About Page for SEO So It Builds Trust and Ranks?

Learn how to optimize an about page for SEO so it builds trust and ranks. Add real team details, clear headings, and honest proof that wins buyers.

Short answer: An about page ranks when it tells the real story of the business in plain words and answers the searches buyers type about the company. Google reads the page for trust signals, such as real names, real photos, and clear contact details. Beginners start by writing the story honestly, then add headings, links, and schema that help Google grasp it.

You write an about page in an afternoon and wonder why it never ranks or wins trust. Most about pages hide behind vague claims and stock photos, and buyers scroll past them without a second thought.

Most businesses fill the about page with praise for themselves and no facts a buyer can check. Readers spot empty praise fast, and Google finds no trust signal to read. Real names, real dates, and real work beat praise every time.

You finish with an about page that proves the business is real, answers buyer doubts, and ranks for searches about your company name.

Tell the Real Story in Plain Words

You learn how to optimize an about page for SEO by starting with the true story of the business. A buyer reads the page to learn who stands behind the work, so honesty matters more than polish. Write the story the way you would tell it to a new client across a table.

Open With Who You Serve and What You Do

The first lines should name the people you serve and the work you do for them. A plumbing firm opens by naming homeowners in its city and the repairs it handles daily. That clear opening helps both the reader and Google grasp the page at once. A clear opening also helps voice searchers who ask about the firm by name.

Add Dates, Places, and Real Milestones

Real dates and places turn a vague story into a checkable one. A firm that names its home city and its founding year says so plainly, and a buyer can trust the claim. Milestones such as a first hire or a new office add proof that the business grows.

Show the People Behind the Business

You apply how to optimize an about page for SEO by showing the real people behind the work. A buyer trusts a face and a name far more than a logo alone. Add a photo and a short line for each key person, and the page starts to feel human.

Add Real Photos, Not Stock Faces

Stock photos of smiling strangers tell the buyer nothing about your team. A real photo of the founder at work proves the business exists and shows pride in the team. Ask each person for a short line about their role, and keep the words in their own voice.

Name Roles So Buyers Know Who Does What

A buyer who needs help wants to know who answers the phone and who does the work. List each role beside each name, from founder to support lead. Clear roles remove doubt, and a buyer who knows the team calls sooner. A buyer who knows the team by name feels more at ease on the first call.

Add Trust Signals Buyers Can Check

You prove how to optimize an about page for SEO when you add trust signals a buyer can verify. A membership, a certificate, or a press mention each gives the buyer a way to check the claim. Unchecked claims fade fast, while verified signals build lasting trust.

List Memberships and Certificates With Links

A trade membership or a certificate gains weight when the buyer can click through and verify it. Link each claim to the body that issued it, so the proof sits one click away. A claim without a link reads as a boast, while a linked claim reads as a fact. Read What Is Topical Authority in SEO and How Do You Build It to see how trust shapes rank across a whole site.

Show Real Work Instead of Empty Praise

A short line about a real project beats a paragraph of praise about quality. Describe one job, the problem it solved, and the result the client saw. Real work gives the buyer a picture of what to expect from your team. A photo of the finished job beside the words makes the story stick.

Shape Headings, Links, and Schema for Search

You complete how to optimize an about page for SEO by shaping the headings, links, and schema around the story. One main heading names the business, and subheadings divide the story into clear parts. Those parts help Google read the page and help buyers scan it fast.

Use One Main Heading With the Business Name

The main heading should carry the business name and the trade in plain words. A heading that names the firm and its city tells Google exactly what the page covers. Subheadings then cover the story, the team, and the values in order. Keep the heading short, so it shows fully in the search results.

Link the About Page to Services and Contact

An about page that links to service pages guides the buyer toward the next step. A reader who trusts the story can click straight to the service they need. Add a clear link to the contact page too, so the buyer never hunts for a way to reach you. Keep the Page Fresh as the Business Grows

You finish how to optimize an about page for SEO by keeping the page fresh as the business grows. A new hire, a new office, or a new service each deserve a line on the page. A stale about page tells buyers the business stopped moving, so update it with each real change.

Update Team Changes the Week They Happen

A new hire or a departure should reach the about page within days, not months. Buyers notice outdated teams, and the mismatch dents trust. Keep a short note of team changes, and fold each one into the page as it happens.

Recheck Links and Details Every Few Months

Memberships lapse, phone numbers change, and office addresses move. A short check every few months catches those shifts before a buyer does. Fix each stale detail at once, and the page keeps proving the business is alive and real. A page with current details shows the buyer that the business pays attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should an about page target a keyword?

Yes, target the business name plus the trade or city, since buyers search for the company by name. A page that ranks for the company name greets buyers who already heard of you. Keep the name natural in the title and the main heading.

How long should an about page be?

An about page should run as long as the true story needs, usually a few short sections. A tiny firm may need only a story, a team, and contact details. A larger firm may add history, values, and milestones, yet each part must earn its place.

Do I need schema on an about page?

Schema helps Google grasp the business details on the page, such as the name and the type of organization. Add Organization markup with the name, logo, and contact details. The markup stays invisible to readers while it clarifies the page for search engines.

Should I add photos of my team?

Yes, real photos of your team build trust faster than any written claim. Buyers like to see who will answer their call and do their work. Ask each person for a photo they like, and keep the images current.

Can an about page rank on Google?

Yes, an about page can rank for searches about the company name and for local trade searches. A page with real details, clear headings, and honest content earns that rank. Link the page from the main menu so Google and buyers both find it fast.

What should I avoid on an about page?

Avoid empty praise, stock photos of strangers, and claims no buyer can check. Vague lines about passion and quality tell the reader nothing. Replace each boast with a fact, a date, or a piece of real work.

Should the about page link to service pages?

Yes, link to the service pages that match the story, so a buyer who trusts you can act at once. A reader who likes the team should reach the service in one click. Add a contact link too, so the next step stays obvious.

How often should I update my about page?

Update the page whenever the team, the office, or the services change. A quick review every few months catches stale details before a buyer does. A page that mirrors the real business keeps trust high.
