What Is the Skyscraper Technique in SEO and How Does It Work?

Learn how the skyscraper technique finds linked pages, builds a taller version, and wins their links with short outreach that site owners answer.

The skyscraper technique means you find a popular page, build a taller version, and win its links for your page. You beat the best page on a topic with better facts. This guide explains what is the skyscraper technique in SEO and how you use it to earn links.

Most link builders copy a popular page and change a few words. They send the same pitch to hundreds of sites. This habit wastes time on pages that add no new value.

You will learn how to find pages with many links and build a stronger version. You will finish with a clear outreach plan that earns links from real sites.

How The Skyscraper Technique Finds Pages Worth Beating

The method starts with a search for pages that already earn many links on your topic. You type your topic into a backlink tool and you list the pages with the most links. A page with many links shows that other writers already trust its content.

You pick one page that you can beat with better facts, fresher data, and clearer steps. A page that covers a topic in a thin way leaves room for a stronger version. A page that already covers the topic in full leaves little room, so you skip it and pick another target.

Brian Dean created the method when he found a popular guide about Google ranking factors. He built a longer guide with more factors and clearer steps. Sites that linked to the old guide moved their links to his new guide because it served their readers better.

How You Build A Page That Beats The Original

You beat the original by adding facts, examples, and steps that the old page lacks. You read the old page twice and you list every gap a reader would still face. You fill those gaps with real examples and clear advice that a reader can use at once.

Many writers ask what is the skyscraper technique in SEO when they first hear the name. They learn that it means building a taller page and winning the links that point to the old page. They then plan their new page around the gaps they found in the original.

A baking blogger found a thin guide about sourdough starters with few steps and no photos. She wrote a full guide with day by day photos and a troubleshooting table for common faults. Bakers linked her guide because it answered the exact problems that the old page left unsolved.

How You Find Sites That Already Link To The Original

You find linkers by entering the old page address into a backlink tool and listing every site that links to it. The tool shows blogs, guides, and resource pages that already care about the topic. These sites form your outreach list because they already link to a page like yours.

You filter the list to sites that still publish and still link out to useful guides. You drop sites that look dead, spammy, or far from your topic. A short list of real sites earns more links than a long list of weak sites.

Ahmed runs a Lahore fitness blog and he found many sites that linked to an old guide about home workouts. He kept only the blogs that still published fresh posts and he emailed each owner with a short note. Several owners moved their links to his stronger guide because it gave their readers clearer steps.

How You Write Outreach That Earns The Link

Your outreach email earns a link when it shows the owner a clear gain for their readers. You name the old page they link to and you point to your stronger version in one line. This short form explains what is the skyscraper technique in SEO in action because it trades a weak link for a better one.

You keep the email short because a busy owner reads it in seconds and decides fast. You praise one part of their site, you link your guide, and you close with a short thanks. This respect for their time lifts your reply rate above long pitches that owners delete unread.

Maryam runs a Karachi craft site and she emailed a blogger who linked to a thin guide about fabric dyes. She named the old guide, she linked her own guide with step photos, and she thanked the blogger in two lines. The blogger replied the next day and swapped the old link for the new guide.

How You Measure Results And Repeat The Method

You track each pitch in a simple sheet with the site name, the date, and the reply you received. This record shows which emails earn links and which emails earn silence. You repeat the winning style and you drop the opening lines that owners ignore.

You also watch your backlink report each month to count the new links that the method earned. A rising count shows that your taller pages pull links from real sites. A flat count tells you to pick stronger targets and write sharper outreach.

Sara runs a Multan food blog and she tracked her skyscraper pitches in one sheet for three months. She found that short emails with one clear gain earned the most links. She kept that form and her site earned new links from food blogs each month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who created the skyscraper technique?

Brian Dean created the method and shared it with marketers through his site Backlinko. He showed how a taller page wins links from sites that link to the old page. His case studies still guide link builders who use the method today.

How long does the skyscraper technique take?

Most campaigns take a few weeks from research to first links. You spend the first days finding targets and building the taller page. You then send outreach over the next weeks and replies arrive as owners read your notes.

Do I need a paid tool to find linked pages?

A paid backlink tool speeds up the search, while free tools still show some linking sites. You can start with free checks and Google searches for popular pages on your topic. You move to a paid tool when your outreach grows and you need full lists.

How is the skyscraper technique different from guest posting?

Guest posting earns a link by writing a new article for another site, while the skyscraper method earns a link by improving a page on your own site. The phrase what is the skyscraper technique in SEO points to this difference because the method builds your own asset instead of borrowing space. You keep the traffic and the links on your own pages.

What makes a page tall enough to win links?

A tall page adds facts, examples, images, and steps that the old page lacks. It answers the follow up questions that readers ask after they read the old page. You know the page is tall enough when a reader gains clear value that the old page never gave.

How many sites should I email for one page?

You should email every real site that links to the old page, even when the list looks long. A focused list of live sites earns more links than a huge list of dead sites. You work through the list in small batches and you track each reply in your sheet.

What if the site owner never replies?

You send one short follow up after a week and then you move on to the next site. Many owners reply to the follow up because the first note slipped past them. You never send a third note because repeated mails annoy owners and harm your name.

Can a new website use the skyscraper technique?

A new website can use the method when its taller page truly beats the old page. You start with small niche topics where the old pages look thin and easy to beat. Each win builds links and trust that open the way to bigger targets.

You now know how to find a linked page, build a taller version, and win its links. You learned what is the skyscraper technique in SEO, how to find linkers, and how to write outreach that owners answer. Keep building stronger pages and keep tracking each win.
