How Do Beginners Use Google Analytics 4 to Find SEO Wins?

Learn how to use Google Analytics 4 for SEO. Find your top pages, spot the pages that lose visitors, and turn data into clear SEO wins with one simple weekly habit.

Short answer: Beginners use Google Analytics 4 for SEO by opening the landing page report, finding the pages that bring search visitors, and spotting the pages that lose those visitors fast. You compare search traffic with other channels, and you track the actions that matter to your business. The data shows you which pages to fix first and which pages to build more of.

Google Analytics 4 shows you what visitors do after they arrive from search. You open the landing page report and find the pages that bring the most visitors. That picture tells you where your next SEO win hides.

Most beginners open Analytics, feel lost in the menus, and close it without learning a thing. They stare at totals that explain nothing and miss the page level detail that drives decisions. One report, read the same way each week, turns the tool from a puzzle into a habit.

You will learn one report at a time and turn numbers into fixes that grow your traffic.

Start With the Landing Page Report

The landing page report shows the first page each visitor saw, so it reveals which pages pull people in from search. You open the report, filter it to search traffic, and sort by visitors. Learning how to use Google Analytics 4 for SEO starts here, because this report ties each visit to the page that earned it. A page high on the list earns its keep, while a page missing from the list needs help or a new angle.

Filter the Report to Search Traffic Only

Search traffic tells a different story from social or direct traffic, so you filter the report to show search visits alone. You click the search channel in the report and watch the list reshape around organic visits. You note the top pages and the actions visitors took on each one. A filtered view keeps your decisions honest, because it stops a viral social post from hiding weak search pages.

A local gym, for example, found its class timetable page brought the most search visits, while its homepage brought far fewer. The owner added class details and signup links to the timetable page first. Signups from search rose, because the page that already attracted visitors now converted them too.

Find the Pages That Bring Visitors but Lose Them

A page that attracts visitors and loses them fast needs a closer look. You pair the landing page report with engagement time to find pages where visitors leave after a few seconds. You learn how to use Google Analytics 4 for SEO by hunting these leaks, because a page that ranks but fails readers wastes the traffic it earns. A short fix, such as a clearer opening or a faster load, often turns a leaky page into a keeper.

Compare Engagement Time Across Similar Pages

You compare engagement time across pages that serve the same kind of search, since a fair comparison needs similar pages. A service page with far lower time than its siblings likely hides a problem, such as a slow load or a confusing opening. You open the weak page yourself and read it as a visitor would. The flaw usually shows itself at once, and you fix it the same day.

A clinic website, for example, found one treatment page with far lower engagement than the rest. The owner opened it and saw a large image that pushed the text below the screen on phones. A smaller image fixed the page, and engagement time rose to match the other pages.

Compare Search Traffic With Other Channels

Search traffic behaves differently from social or paid traffic, so you compare channels side by side. You open the traffic report and look at visitors, engagement, and actions for each channel. You learn how to use Google Analytics 4 for SEO by asking which channel brings readers who stay and act. Search visitors often arrive with a clear need, so they convert better than visitors who arrived from a casual scroll.

Ask Which Channel Brings Visitors Who Act

You look beyond visitor counts and check which channel leads to calls, form fills, and signups. A channel with fewer visitors but more actions beats a channel with many visitors and no actions. You shift effort toward the channel that brings buyers, not browsers.

Track the Actions That Matter to Your Business

Visits mean little when no one acts, so you track the actions that pay your bills. You set up events for calls, form submissions, and trial signups in Analytics. You learn how to use Google Analytics 4 for SEO by tying each ranking page to the actions it drives. A page that brings many visitors but no actions needs a better offer, while a page with few visitors and many actions deserves more promotion.

Mark Calls and Form Fills as Key Events

You mark each valuable action as a key event, so Analytics counts it clearly in every report. You test each event yourself by calling the number and submitting the form. You check that the count rises after your test, since a broken event hides real results. A working event turns a vague traffic report into a clear picture of what earns money.

A law firm, for example, marked consultation form fills as a key event and found one guide drove most of them. The firm wrote two more guides on the same topic and linked them to the first one. Form fills from search grew, because the firm fed the topic that already worked.

Pair GA4 With Search Console for the Full Picture

Analytics shows behaviour, while Search Console shows the search side of the same visits. You read them together to see which queries bring visitors and what those visitors do next. You learn how to use Google Analytics 4 for SEO by joining the two views, because neither tool tells the whole story alone. A query with many clicks but low engagement points at a page that needs work, while a query with few clicks and high engagement points at a page that deserves more reach.

Match Queries to Pages Each Week

You open Search Console, note the top queries, and find the page each query lands on. You then open Analytics and check what visitors did on those pages. You fix the pages where clicks arrive and engagement drops, and you promote the pages where engagement runs high. This weekly habit keeps your SEO work tied to real behaviour, not to guesses.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Google Analytics 4?

Google Analytics 4 is a free tool that shows what visitors do on your website after they arrive. It tracks pages viewed, time spent, and actions such as calls and form fills. You use it to learn which pages work and which ones need fixing.

How do I see SEO traffic in Google Analytics 4?

Open the traffic acquisition report and look for the organic search channel. That channel shows visits that arrived from search engines without paid ads. Filter by that channel whenever you want to judge SEO alone.

What is the landing page report in GA4?

The landing page report lists the first page each visitor saw on your site. It shows visits, engagement, and actions for each landing page. You use it to find the pages that pull visitors in from search.

How do I track form fills in GA4?

You set up an event that fires when a visitor submits a form, then you mark that event as a key event. Test the form yourself and check that the count rises after your test. A working event lets you see which pages drive real enquiries.

Why does GA4 show different numbers from Search Console?

The two tools count different things, so their numbers never match exactly. Search Console counts clicks from search results, while Analytics counts visits that loaded your pages. Use Search Console for queries and clicks, and use Analytics for behaviour after arrival.

How often should I check GA4 for SEO?

Check the landing page report once a week, so you can spot changes while they stay fresh. A weekly habit beats a monthly deep dive, because small problems show up early. Pair the check with Search Console for the full picture.

What is a key event in Google Analytics 4?

A key event is an action you mark as valuable, such as a call, a form fill, or a trial signup. Analytics highlights key events in its reports, so you can see which pages drive them. Mark only the actions that matter to your business.

Can GA4 show which keywords bring visitors?

GA4 shows very little keyword detail, because search engines hide most query data for privacy. You should use Search Console for keyword and query data instead. Pair the two tools, so queries from Search Console meet behaviour from Analytics.
