What Are Informational Keywords in SEO and How Do You Find Them?

Informational keywords show a reader who wants to learn rather than buy. Learn how to spot them, find them with free tools, and turn them into guides that rank.

Short answer: Informational keywords are search phrases that show a reader wants to learn rather than buy. The reader types a question, and the reader wants a clear answer rather than a sales page. A guide that answers the question well can rank for the phrase and earn trust.

Most beginners target informational keywords with sales pages and wonder why the pages never rank. A sales page answers a buying question, and the searcher asked a learning question. The mismatch pushes the page down, and the reader clicks a guide instead.

You will learn how to spot informational keywords, find them with free tools, and turn them into guides that rank.

What Informational Keywords Look Like

People ask what are informational keywords in SEO when they first sort keywords by intent, and the answer is simple. An informational keyword shows a reader who wants to learn, and it usually starts with a question word or a how to phrase. Phrases like how to fix a leaking tap, what is a mortgage, and why does my phone overheat all show learning intent.

A commercial keyword shows a reader who wants to buy, and it usually names a product, a price, or a brand. Phrases like best running shoes under 100 dollars, buy noise cancelling headphones, and dentist near me all show buying intent. The same topic can hold both kinds of phrases, and the intent decides which page you write for each one. You never send a learner to a sales page, and you never send a buyer to a long guide.

A home repair blog ranked for a phrase about fixing a leaking tap within two months. The blog held no links and no fame, and it won because no strong site had answered that exact phrase with a clear guide. The post brought steady visits, and those visits led readers to the rest of the blog. One specific win opened the door to the next one, and the blog grew from answers rather than ads.

How You Find Informational Keywords With Free Tools

You find them with free tools once you know what are informational keywords in SEO and how they differ from buying phrases. You start with one broad topic, and you type it into Google to read the autocomplete suggestions. Google shows the phrases that real people type after your seed words, and the question phrases in that list form your first set of informational keywords.

Read Autocomplete and People Also Ask

The People Also Ask box shows the questions that searchers ask next, and each question hides an informational keyword inside it. You open the box, click three questions, and read the new questions that appear below them. You note the questions that match your topic, and you turn each one into a phrase for your list. Those questions show real demand, and they show you the exact words that searchers use.

Mine Search Console for Real Queries

Search Console shows the real queries that already bring your pages into results, even when your rank sits on page three. You open the performance report, sort by impressions, and read the long phrases that appear near the bottom. A page that already appears for a question needs only a better section on that question to climb. You build that section, and you watch the phrase rise over the next month. You start from seed words, and you can learn that method in What Are Seed Keywords in SEO and How Do You Find Them?.

How You Tell Informational Intent From Buying Intent

You tell the two apart by reading the results page for the phrase. A phrase that shows guides, how to articles, and videos holds informational intent, and a phrase that shows product pages, prices, and ads holds buying intent. The results page tells you what Google thinks the searcher wants, and you trust that verdict over your own guess. You write the page type that the results page shows, and you never fight the verdict.

A phrase about cheap flights to Paris may bring many searches, and it deserves no guide on a site about home gardening. The readers would leave at once, and the page would help no other page on the site. You pick phrases that sit inside your niche, and you let the off topic phrases go no matter how tempting they look. The intent test for every phrase follows What Is Search Intent in SEO.

How You Turn an Informational Keyword Into a Guide That Ranks

You turn the phrase into a guide by answering the question in the first lines and expanding it with detail below. A searcher who types a long question wants the answer fast, and a page that delays the answer loses that searcher. You give the direct answer first, and you follow it with steps, examples, and honest detail that a short answer cannot hold. That shape earns the click, the stay, and the rank.

You place the phrase in the title, in the first paragraph, and in one heading, and you use it naturally nowhere else. Searchers see their own words in the title, and they trust the page at once. You write the rest of the page in plain words that cover the topic fully, and you never force the phrase into sentences where it sounds wrong. Natural use beats repetition, and readers stay longer on a page that reads like a human wrote it.

People who learn what are informational keywords in SEO often collect a huge list and write nothing. You pick five phrases this week, and you write one guide for each of them. Five focused guides beat a list of 500 phrases, and the five guides teach you which phrases bring readers. You grow the list from the winners, and you keep writing.

Answers First, Sales Later

Informational keywords give a new site a fair fight. You find real phrases with free tools, judge them by intent, and answer each one with a focused guide. Each win adds trust, and the trust lifts your next page a little higher. The reader who learns from you today may buy from you tomorrow, and the guide is the bridge between the two.

When someone asks you what are informational keywords in SEO, you can show them the question phrases, the free tools, and the five guides you built from them. That proof beats any tool pitch, and it works on any budget. You build the next five guides from the winners, and you keep the list growing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are informational keywords in SEO?

Informational keywords are search phrases that show a reader wants to learn rather than buy. The reader types a question or a how to phrase, and the reader wants a clear answer rather than a sales page. A guide that answers the question well can rank for the phrase and earn trust.

How are informational keywords different from commercial keywords?

Informational keywords show a reader who wants to learn, and commercial keywords show a reader who wants to buy. A phrase like how to fix a leaking tap holds learning intent, and a phrase like best running shoes under 100 dollars holds buying intent. You write a guide for the first phrase, and you write a product page for the second one.

How do I find informational keywords for free?

You start from a broad topic, read Google autocomplete and People Also Ask, and check the real pages that already rank. Search Console shows the real queries that already bring your pages into results. You collect the question phrases, and you judge each one by intent before you write.

Do informational keywords bring sales?

Informational keywords bring readers who want to learn, and those readers rarely buy on the first visit. The guide earns their trust, and a share of those readers returns later to buy. You measure the win in trust and return visits, and you never judge a guide by first day sales alone.

How many informational keywords should one page target?

One page should target one main informational phrase and a few close variants that ask the same question. Two phrases that ask different questions deserve two pages. You group phrases by intent first, and you write one page per intent. That grouping stops thin pages, and it keeps your site clean.

Can a new website rank for informational keywords?

Yes. Informational phrases carry less competition than buying phrases, and a new site can win them with one focused guide. Big sites chase the buying terms, and they leave the specific questions open for small sites. Each win builds trust, and that trust later lifts your harder pages too.

Where do I place an informational keyword in an article?

You write the rest of the page in plain words, and you never force the phrase into sentences where it sounds wrong.

How do I know if an informational keyword is winnable?

You open the top five results for the phrase, and you judge their depth in five minutes. A phrase with weak thin pages on top is winnable, and a phrase with strong detailed pages on top is a fight for later. You also check that the phrase matches your niche and brings readers who want what you offer.
