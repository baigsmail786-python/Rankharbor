What Is Structured Data in SEO and How Do Beginners Add Schema to a Website?

Learn what is structured data in SEO for beginners, how schema markup works, and copy two JSON-LD blocks for FAQ and Article pages step by step.

Most beginners skip structured data because the code looks hard, and they miss rich results that rivals already show. They lose clicks to pages with stars and FAQs in the search results.

What Structured Data Is and How Google Reads It

Structured data is a small block of code that labels the parts of a page in a way Google can read. You tell Google that a line is a question, a date is a publish date, and a name is an author. Google reads those labels, and it uses them to show rich results that stand out in search. You add the code once per page, and it keeps working without further care.

Schema Markup in Plain Words

Schema markup is a shared vocabulary that sites use to label their content for search engines. You write it in a format called JSON-LD, and you place it inside a script tag on the page. Each label names a type, such as Article or FAQPage, and each type holds fields like headline, date, and author. You learn what is structured data in SEO for beginners by reading one simple block and changing its words to match your own page.

What Rich Results Look Like in Search

Rich results are search listings that show extra detail, such as FAQs, stars, or event dates below the title. A page with FAQ schema can show its questions right on the results page, and that extra space draws the eye. A recipe page can show a photo, a rating, and a cooking time before the reader clicks. A local plumber page with FAQ schema can answer the price question before the call, so the clicks it wins are warmer.

Which Schema Types a Beginner Needs First

A beginner needs only two schema types at first, and those two cover most pages on a small site. FAQ schema fits any page that answers questions, and Article schema fits every blog post and guide. You add both to a page that holds a guide with questions, and you add Article schema alone to a plain post. You learn the two blocks well, and you add more types only when a page truly needs them.

FAQ Schema for Pages That Answer Questions

FAQ schema marks each question and answer on your page, and Google may show them under your listing. You write the questions your readers actually ask, and you answer each one in two or three plain sentences. You keep the answers honest, and you match them word for word with the visible FAQ on the page. A dental clinic page that marks its price and booking questions can win extra space in the results for local searches.

Article Schema for Blog Posts and Guides

Article schema tells Google that a page is a written piece, and it names the headline, the author, and the publish date. You add it to every blog post and guide, because it helps Google file the page in the right place. You fill in the headline with the exact title, the author with your name or brand, and the date with the real publish date. A guide that carries Article schema looks complete to Google, and it stands a better chance of showing rich details.

You test every page you mark up, and you fix the errors the tool reports. You repeat the same steps for each new page, and the routine soon takes only minutes.

Two Copy Paste Blocks and How to Fix Common Errors

You can reuse two blocks for most pages, and this section gives you both in words you can copy. The FAQ block lists your questions and answers, and the Article block names the headline, author, and date. You copy each block, change the words to match your page, and paste it into the page head. The FAQ Block You Can Reuse

The FAQ block opens with the FAQPage type, and it holds one entry for each question on your page. Each entry names the question in a field called name, and it holds the answer in a field called acceptedAnswer. You write the question and answer exactly as they appear on the visible page, and you add one entry per question. You keep the answers short, and you never mark up questions that the page does not show.

The Article Block and the Errors to Fix

Frequently Asked Questions

What is structured data?

Structured data is code that labels the parts of a page so Google can read its meaning. You mark questions, dates, authors, and other facts with a shared vocabulary called schema markup. Google uses those labels to show rich results, and your page stands out more in search.

Does structured data improve rankings directly?

Structured data does not raise rankings on its own, because Google treats it as a label rather than a ranking signal. It can still win you more clicks, because rich results draw the eye and answer questions before the click. You add it for visibility, and you win rankings with strong content and links.

What is the difference between structured data and schema markup?

Structured data is the idea of labelling page content for machines, and schema markup is the shared vocabulary you use to do it. You write structured data with schema markup, and you place it in a format called JSON-LD. The two terms describe the same job from two sides, and you will see both in guides.

Which schema type should I add first?

You add FAQ schema first when your page answers questions, because it can win extra space in the results fast. You add Article schema first when your page is a blog post or guide with no questions. Most guides carry both, and you add them together when the page holds a FAQ section.

Do I need a plugin to add structured data?

You do not need a plugin when your site uses plain HTML, because you can paste the block by hand. You will find a plugin easier on WordPress, because it writes the code from the fields you fill in. You pick the route that fits your site, and you test the result either way.

How do I test my structured data?

You test structured data with the Google Rich Results Test, and the tool reads your page and lists what it found. You paste the page address, run the test, and read the errors and warnings it reports. You fix each error, rerun the test, and publish only when the test shows no errors.

Can wrong structured data hurt my site?

Wrong structured data can hurt your site when it misleads Google, because Google may drop your rich results or flag the page. You keep every label honest, and you mark up only facts the page truly shows. You test each page after you add code, and you fix errors the same day.

Does structured data help with AI search?

Structured data helps AI search, because AI tools read labelled facts more easily than plain text. A page with clear FAQ and Article labels gives an AI answer engine clean facts to quote. You add schema now, and your pages stay ready as AI search grows.
