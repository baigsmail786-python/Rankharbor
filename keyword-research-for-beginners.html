Keyword Research for Beginners: From Seed List to Content Plan

Keyword research is a content plan, not a spreadsheet. Learn the beginner-friendly process: seeds, intent, clusters, competitor gaps and prioritization.

What keyword research actually is

Keyword research finds the words and questions your audience types into search engines, then turns them into a plan for pages. Done well, it tells you what to create, in what order, and which existing page should target what — so your pages stop competing with each other.

Step 1: Build a seed list

Start from your products, your customers' problems and the language buyers use (sales calls and support tickets are gold). Expand seeds with autocomplete, 'People also ask', related searches and keyword tools. Do not filter yet — collect widely first.

Step 2: Classify by search intent

Every keyword carries an intent:

  • Informational — learn something ('how to reduce churn')
  • Commercial — compare options ('best invoicing tools for freelancers')
  • Transactional — act now ('invoicing software pricing')
  • Navigational — find a brand or page

Match the page type to the intent

Intent decides the page type. A blog post cannot satisfy a transactional query well, and a product page is the wrong answer to an informational one. Label every keyword before going further.

Step 3: Group into topic clusters

Cluster keywords that share intent and could be answered by one strong page or a hub-and-spoke set. One cluster = one primary page. This prevents cannibalization and mirrors how topical authority is built.

Step 4: Check competitors and prioritize

Look at which competitors rank for each cluster and what their pages do well. Then prioritize by business value first, realistic difficulty second, and volume last — volume estimates are directional, not promises. The output is a content map: cluster, target page, intent, priority. That map is exactly what our keyword research service delivers, ready to execute for quarters.
