How to Evaluate Backlink Quality: A 9-Point Checklist

Domain metrics alone do not make a link good. Use this nine-point checklist — relevance, traffic signals, editorial standards, link patterns and more — before you accept any placement.

Why metrics alone mislead

A single authority score is the easiest number to game and the least reliable way to judge a link. Sites with impressive metrics can be link farms in disguise, while a small niche publication with modest metrics can send a link worth ten of theirs. Evaluate the whole picture.

The 9-point checklist

Work through these checks for every candidate placement:

  • 1. Topical relevance — does the site (and the specific page) cover your niche or your buyers' world?
  • 2. Real audience — are there signs of genuine readership: engagement, an editorial voice, a real team?
  • 3. Traffic signals — does the site appear to receive organic traffic for relevant topics?
  • 4. Editorial standards — would this article exist if nobody paid for links?
  • 5. Outbound link patterns — do articles link naturally to varied sources, or sell a link in every post?
  • 6. Indexation — is the linking page indexed and capable of ranking?
  • 7. Anchor-text profile — does the site's outbound anchor text look natural or stuffed?
  • 8. Site health — no hacked pages, spun content, or casino/pharma neighbours in the link profile
  • 9. Placement context — will your link sit in the main content, read by humans, not in a footer or author-box farm?

Red flags that end the conversation

Walk away from sites that publish on any topic for a fee, guarantee placement without editorial review, show thousands of unrelated outbound links, or cannot show you real published examples. Also be wary of brand-new sites with suspiciously perfect metrics.

Applying the checklist in practice

Score each candidate against the nine points and record the reasoning. In our campaigns, this evaluation happens before a publisher is ever proposed to a client — see how we evaluate publishers on the SaaS link building page. A smaller list of placements that pass all nine checks will outperform a larger list that passes three.
