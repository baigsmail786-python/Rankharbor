What Is SaaS Link Building? A Practical Guide for Software Companies

SaaS link building earns backlinks from software, B2B and industry publications to strengthen product and content pages. Here is how it works and what makes a SaaS link valuable.

The short answer

SaaS link building is the process of earning hyperlinks from other websites — SaaS blogs, B2B publications, review platforms, partner sites and industry communities — that point to a software company's website. Search engines treat these links as endorsements, and for competitive SaaS keywords they are often the deciding factor between page one and page three.

Unlike generic link building, SaaS link building is shaped by how software is bought: long research cycles, comparison searches ('X vs Y', 'best tools for…') and product-led content. The links that matter most point at product, feature, integration and high-value content pages — from sites your buyers already trust.

Why SaaS companies need links more than most

Most SaaS categories have a handful of well-funded incumbents with years of accumulated authority. A newer product can publish excellent content and still stall on page two because its domain lacks the link authority competitors have built. Links close that gap: they raise the ceiling for every page on the domain, not just the page linked to.

Links also drive referral traffic from publications your audience reads — visitors who often convert better than cold search traffic because they arrive with context and trust.

What a good SaaS backlink looks like

Quality in SaaS link building comes down to four checks:

  • Topical relevance — the linking site covers software, your industry, or your buyers' problems
  • Editorial legitimacy — a real publication with standards, not a link farm or paid-post mill
  • Page fit — the linking article genuinely relates to your target page
  • Natural placement — in-content, with sensible anchor text, surrounded by relevant copy

Common SaaS link-building tactics

The tactics that consistently work for SaaS include guest posting on industry publications, link insertions into existing relevant articles, digital PR around original data or research, integration and partner pages, podcast and community appearances, and creating linkable assets such as free tools, templates and benchmark reports.

What does not work for long: buying bulk links, private blog networks, and irrelevant directory submissions. These can create risk without durable ranking benefit.

How to start

Start with a link-gap analysis: which sites link to your top three competitors but not to you? That list, filtered for relevance and quality, is your first outreach target list. Pair it with one or two linkable assets and a steady guest-posting cadence, and you have a SaaS link-building programme.
